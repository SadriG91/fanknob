Your Mac's fans, on a knob.

Quiet when you're reading. Cold when you're compiling. A menu-bar app for Apple Silicon that puts your fans where you want them — or hands them to a temperature curve and lets them track the heat.

The app

The fan turns accent-colored whenever you're overriding the firmware.

What it does

One knob, every fan

Every fan has its own RPM range, so fanknob maps that range to 0–100. Fifty means halfway, whatever machine you're on. Link the fans together or drive them separately.

Curves that follow the heat

Quiet, Balanced and Turbo presets, or your own °C:% points. Re-checked every two seconds against a smoothed CPU average.

A limit you can't overshoot

While you're overriding the fans, fanknob watches the temperature and hands them back to the firmware above 95 °C. Adjustable, and it stays handed back until you ask again.

A terminal, if you'd rather

Set a speed with fanknob set 40, pick a curve with fanknob preset quiet, or watch it live in fanknob tui. No sudo per command.

One thing worth knowing. A fixed speed means you own thermal management, not your Mac's firmware. The 95 °C limit is the backstop, but prefer a curve if you're leaving an override on — a curve responds to heat, a fixed speed doesn't. Return to auto when you're done, or let a timed hold expire on its own. (MacBook Air is fanless; there's nothing to control there.)

Help

Troubleshooting

"helper not installed", or the status light is orange
The background helper isn't running, so fanknob can read your fans but not change them. Reinstalling fixes it. To start it by hand: 
sudo launchctl bootstrap system /Library/LaunchDaemons/com.fanknob.daemon.plist
The fans are stuck at one speed
Hand them back to the firmware: 
fanknob auto
Curves and presets do nothing
Those need the helper running — it's what re-evaluates the curve over time. See above.
Don't mix install methods
Use the installer package (or the Homebrew cask) or a build from source, not both — two helpers would fight over the same fans, so the second one refuses to start. brew uninstall --cask fanknob removes the first.
Still stuck?
The helper logs to /var/log/fanknobd.log, and fanknob status prints what it thinks is going on. If that doesn't explain it, open an issue.
